Against deliberation

Against Democracy, Jason Brennan

  • "Hobbits are mostly apathetic and ignorant about politics. Hooligans are the rabid sports fans of politics."
  • Corruption: "High-status individuals talk more, are perceived as more accurate and credible, and have more influence, regardless of whether the high-status individuals actually know more." (Mendelberg, cited by Brennan)

Citizens' assemblies

1

50–150 people; stratified lottery

2

learn from experts and stakeholders

3

deliberate in facilitated small groups over several weekends

4

vote on recommendations; secret ballot

British Columbia in 2004: members spend a year on electoral reform; the proposal goes straight to a referendum, gets 58%, and fails (60% needed).

Ireland between 2016 and 2018: 99 members recommend changing the abortion law; the 2018 referendum passes it with 66%.

Climate Assembly UK in early 2020: 108 members over six weekends, commissioned by six parliamentary committees, run by Involve with the Sortition Foundation and mySociety; advisory only.

France's Citizens' Convention for Climate, between 2019 and 2020: 150 members make 149 proposals; Macron promises to pass them but only 24 are fully applied; promised referendum never happens.

Deliberative Polls

James S. Fishkin, When the People Speak

1

random representative group of a few hundred people

2

first questionnaire on issue (before)

3

balanced briefings, small moderated groups, questions to competing experts

4

second questionnaire on issue (after), same questions, answers confidential

Texas energy polls (1996–99): support for paying more for renewables rose from 52% to 84%; by 2007 Texas led the US in wind power.

First held in Britain, 1994, on crime (When the People Speak, p. 96).

More than 100 polls in over 50 countries. Since 2020 often online, with an automated moderator (Stanford Online Deliberation Platform).

Fishkin's trilemma

James S. Fishkin, When the People Speak, ch. 2, “The Trilemma of Democratic Reform”, p. 46

Attempts to realize any two will undermine the achievement of the third:

  • Deliberation: people weigh arguments
  • Political equality: every citizen's view counts the same
  • Mass participation: bulk of the population

join.gov.tw

  • Taiwanese government's online participation platform.
  • Anyone can post a policy proposal.
  • With 5,000 signatures in 60 days, the relevant ministry must respond within about two months.
  • Draft regulations are posted for public comment.
  • A student's 2017 proposal to ban plastic straws passed the threshold; restrictions began in 2019.

Community Notes

Wojcik et al. 2022

1

volunteers write notes on posts on twitter.com

2

a note is shown only if raters who usually disagree both rate it helpful

Both Community Notes and Polis use matrix factorisation. Polis sorts people into opinion groups; Community Notes places each rater on a sliding scale (roughly politically left–right).

Began as Birdwatch in 2021 at Twitter. In 2025 Meta adopted X's algorithm to replace its US fact-checkers; TikTok launched Footnotes.

People who see a note are significantly less likely to reshare the post.

Over 90% of notes are never shown; most are shown after the post has already spread.

Decidim & CONSUL Democracy

Decidim

  • Built by Barcelona City Council in 2016. The name is Catalan for "we decide".
  • Proposals, participatory budgets, debates, surveys and assemblies.
  • Used by Barcelona, the Catalan government, Helsinki, Zurich, Lucerne, the French Senate and National Assembly, and the EU's Conference on the Future of Europe.
  • Since 2019 run by an independent association; no longer depends on Barcelona's politics.
  • June 2026: agreement with New York City, the first local government outside Catalonia to formally partner with the project.

CONSUL Democracy

  • Built by Madrid City Council for its "Decide Madrid" site in 2015.
  • Anyone can make a proposal. If it gets support from 1% of residents over 16 (about 27,000 people), it goes to a city-wide vote.
  • Participatory budgets: residents proposed and voted on how to spend up to €100 million a year.
  • Used by over 100 institutions in 35 countries, including Buenos Aires, Porto Alegre.
  • The left-wing city government that built it lost the 2019 election.

Talk to the City

https://talktothe.city/

1

takes a large set of comments: Polis statements, survey answers, interview transcripts

2

a language model pulls out the individual claims and groups them into topics and subtopics

3

produces an interactive report; every point links back to the original quotes

Summarises; does not try to reach agreement.

Built for the people running a consultation.

Every claim and topic is linked to the original comments it came from. Click a point in the summary and you see the exact words people wrote.

The Habermas Machine

Google DeepMind · Tessler et al., “AI can help humans find common ground in democratic deliberation”, Science, 2024

1

each person in a small group writes their opinion on a policy question

2

the model drafts several group statements that try to capture the common ground

3

participants rank them; the top one is shown

4

participants write critiques; the model drafts a revised statement

Named after Jürgen Habermas, the philosopher of deliberation.

People preferred its statements to a human mediator's 56% of the time. Groups also moved closer together.

Participants were recruited in the UK, partly through the Sortition Foundation, including a small "virtual citizens' assembly".

Built for one study, the Science paper in 2024. DeepMind said it had no immediate plans to release it publicly.
